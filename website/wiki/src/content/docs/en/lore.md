## The Wulpische Lande

They say the continent was once united under a single crown.

Far in the south, where the wind drives the dunes of Valesos across the plains, lay the ancient cities. Their walls still rise out of the sand, vast and pale. Their once golden roofs are said to have shone so brightly even in the midday sun that travellers believed the sun itself was burning inside them. No one knows any more who had them built. Some claim they were the works of forgotten kings, others ascribe them to a people that has long lain buried in the sand.

![A city of domes and minarets at the edge of the desert](/img/lore/lore-1.jpg)

But the cities are still alive.

Merchants from all over the world pass through their harbours. The Khoorer of Valesos have managed to extract the treasures of the steppe and the old ruins, and carry them to distant lands for their wealth. Gold changes hands, and so do rumours, and in the taverns of the trading ports you hear stories of lands that no traveller has ever seen.

![Knights fighting beneath red banners](/img/lore/lore-2.jpg)

In the centre of the continent the wide grassland begins.

Once an empire stretched from the southern plains to the icy fjords of the north. Wulfgar the Terrible, as the chronicles call him, is said to have ruled his subjects with an iron hand. Kingdoms fell beneath his banner, cities opened their gates and whole peoples swore him their loyalty.

But no realm is built for eternity.

Wulfgar's empire broke apart after his death. His successors quarrelled over the crown, his governors declared themselves kings and his old roads fell into disrepair. What had once been a single realm became a patchwork of principalities, castles and quarrelling kingdoms, what we know today as Mesyrna.

The walls of the great cities still stand there. Their towers have been razed, their halls are cold, and grass grows over the old imperial roads. Nobles sit on thrones that once belonged to governors. Courtly power games and intrigue are the order of the day, and they all wrestle with each other for power and influence and argue over old borders that have long been forgotten.

![Nordmarers storming ashore from their ships onto a beach](/img/lore/lore-3.jpg)

And while they quarrel and fight each other, the Nordmarers come.

High up, beyond the wooded valleys and the black mountains, where the fjords cut deep into the land, live the tribes of the north. They are rough, and proud. They know neither the rich trade of Valesos nor the courtly manners of Mesyrna. Their houses stand of stone and wood between snow and rock, and their ships lie in the cold waters of the fjords.

For generations they have looked down on the lands of the south.

Now they come for their riches.

![Map of the Wulpische Lande with Nordmar, Mesyrna, Valesos and the strait](/img/lore/lore-4.jpg)

When the horns of the Nordmarers sound over the passes, the people of Mesyrna know what follows. Villages burn. Border castles fall. Merchants vanish on the old roads. And while the nobles of Mesyrna argue over who is entitled to the crown, the tribes of the north push further and further south.

![The ruins of an ancient temple](/img/lore/lore-5.jpg)

## The island of Myralon

In the strait off the continent lies Myralon, an island that people have avoided for centuries. A mighty volcano rises on it, but its crater has long since gone cold. Only the rugged slopes still remind you that fire once burst from the earth here.

More than a thousand years ago King Mykolos is said to have claimed this island for himself and named it Myralon. There he had a mighty temple built. Mykolos was described as powerful and immeasurably rich, but his greatest wish was to become his downfall as well.

According to legend, the king one day performed a secret ritual with which he wanted to escape his approaching death. No one knows what really happened that night. All that is certain is that the volcano awoke. Fire, ash and molten rock poured over the island. The temple was buried under stone, and with it Mykolos vanished from history.

![The stronghold on Myralon, a ruined tower above a gorge](/img/lore/lore-6.jpg)

Centuries later Emperor Wulfgar the Terrible built a penal colony on the ruins. His advisers suspected rich ore deposits deep underground. Wulfgar's war to subjugate the continent swallowed vast amounts of raw materials. Prisoners from all parts of the continent were to bring the treasures out of the rock.

But Wulfgar had underestimated people.

After a few years the convicts rose up against their overseers. They took the stronghold, drove out the soldiers and made Myralon their home. The former prisoners became outlaws and finally pirates. For generations their brood is said to have lived in the ruins of the old fortress.

![Two settlers look from a hill onto the coast of Myralon, where ships are landing](/img/lore/lore-7.jpg)

So Myralon fell into oblivion. The volcano was considered unpredictable, the island cursed, and stories of pirates and hidden treasure kept even daring adventurers away.

Until this very day.

For now a merchant has decided to take the risk. On his own initiative he sends the first ships to Myralon and begins to build a new settlement. He has it proclaimed in all lands that every baron, craftsman, landless knight or exile is welcome.

He promises land, wealth and a new future.

Where all this will lead, we shall see.
